Why PAN Card Photo Specifications Matter

The Income Tax Department processes millions of PAN applications every year. To automate the workflow, the system validates uploaded photos programmatically. If your photo doesn't match the preset criteria — wrong pixel dimensions, excessive file size, or incorrect format — the system automatically rejects it.

Common problems include uploading a photo that is too large (exceeding 50 KB), using the wrong format (PNG instead of JPEG), or having a coloured or patterned background that fails the background detection check.

PAN Card Photo: Technical Details

Pixel Dimensions vs Physical Size

The PAN card photo requirement is 200 pixels wide by 276 pixels tall at 200 DPI (dots per inch). This translates to a physical size of approximately 25 mm × 35 mm — similar to a standard passport photo but with lower DPI requirements.

Why 50 KB File Size Limit?

The NSDL and UTIITSL portals limit photo uploads to 50 KB to ensure fast processing and minimal server load. This is a strict limit — files even 1 KB over will be rejected. While this makes the photo quite compressed, it is sufficient for identification purposes at the low resolution required.

JPEG Format Only

The PAN card portal accepts only JPEG/JPG format. PNG files, while offering better quality, produce larger file sizes that typically exceed the 50 KB limit. Our tool automatically converts your photo to JPEG when you select the PAN Card preset.

PAN Photo Tips for a Smooth Application

Here are some practical tips to ensure your PAN card photo is accepted on the first attempt:

  • Use a recent photo — should be no more than 6 months old
  • Face the camera directly with both eyes open and a neutral expression
  • Avoid wearing sunglasses or tinted glasses — clear prescription glasses are acceptable but avoid glare
  • Keep the background plain — white or off-white without any patterns, curtains, or shadows
  • Ensure even lighting — no harsh shadows on your face or behind you
  • No hats or head coverings unless for religious reasons, and even then the face must be fully visible from forehead to chin

How PAN Card Photo Differs from Other Documents

Unlike a passport (which requires 35×45 mm at 300 DPI) or a voter ID (also 35×45 mm but colour), the PAN card photo has a unique combination of a small pixel size and tight file size limit. This often confuses applicants who try to use the same photo they used for their passport or voter ID. Each government form has its own distinct photo specifications, and it's important to generate the correct version for each application.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact PAN card photo size in pixels? The exact PAN card photo size is 200 pixels wide × 276 pixels tall at 200 DPI. This is equivalent to 25 mm × 35 mm in physical size.
Can I use a selfie for PAN card application? While possible, selfies often have poor lighting and incorrect framing. It is better to use a proper passport-style photograph taken against a plain white wall.
Why is my PAN card photo being rejected repeatedly? Most rejections happen due to three reasons: incorrect pixel dimensions, file size exceeding 50 KB, or a non-white background. Use ilovephoto.in to check your photo against all these criteria before uploading.
What is the minimum DPI required for PAN card photo? The minimum resolution required is 200 DPI. However, 300 DPI or higher is acceptable as long as the pixel dimensions and file size remain within limits.
Is a PAN card photo different from a passport photo? Yes. PAN card requires 200×276 px at 200 DPI (max 50 KB), while a passport requires 630×810 px at 300 DPI (max 100 KB). They are not interchangeable.
Can I wear glasses in my PAN card photo? Clear prescription glasses are allowed, but avoid sunglasses, tinted lenses, or frames that cover your eyes. Make sure there is no glare on the lenses.
